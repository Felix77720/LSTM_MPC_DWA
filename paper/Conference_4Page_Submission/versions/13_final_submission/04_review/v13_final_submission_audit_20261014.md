# Final submission audit — 2026-10-14

**Release status: ready as a bounded simulation-only paper.** The released claim is that shared LSTM input improves the tested coupled MPC–DWA controller over its DWA-only forecast ablation and local latest-position-hold reconstruction. The evidence does not establish that it is better overall than standalone DWA or MPC.

## Evidence and claim audit

- The main paper reports the completed historical five-controller comparison: 500 scenes × 3 matched seeds × 5 controllers = 7,500 runs, with a 1-s DWA screen and a 30-step MPC horizon. Shared LSTM achieved 936/1,500 collision-free arrivals (62.4%); integrated DWA-only LSTM achieved 836/1,500 (55.7%). The paired gain is 6.7 percentage points (95% scene-cluster CI 4.2–9.2). Against the project-local latest-position-hold MPC–DWA reconstruction, shared LSTM achieved 62.4% versus 2.2% (paired difference 60.2 points, 95% CI 56.2–64.2). The reconstruction is not described as an exact reproduction of the cited source paper.
- The aggregate ranking is retained without qualification by selection: standalone DWA+LSTM achieved 82.4% and standalone MPC+LSTM 95.2%, both above shared MPC–DWA at 62.4%. The 76.4% standalone-DWA value from the earlier v08 implementation is kept separate from the later 82.4% fairness rerun; they use distinct emergency-screen rules and are not pooled. Accordingly, the paper calls shared LSTM the best tested *coupled MPC–DWA variant*, not the best controller overall.
- Scene 404, seed 2 is identified only as a matched route illustration. Only the shared method arrived collision-free in that example; the caption points to aggregate rates and does not generalize from the case. Each route terminates at its recorded terminal state. The collision marker denotes swept contact somewhere along the route, not necessarily at the endpoint.
- The 4,320 Stage-1 development episodes are included as useful calibration evidence: six candidate weight vectors × five integrated branches × 48 development scenes × three matched seeds. Recovery independently revalidated all 4,320 existing records and generated zero new simulations. The selected vector is `[0.15, 0.15, 0.15, 0.55]`, with equal-arm macro-average development collision-free-arrival score 0.8042. At that vector, branch rates are shared LSTM 0.8958, CV-coupled MPC–DWA 0.7431, LSTM-to-MPC-only 0.8194, LSTM-to-DWA-only 0.8958, and latest-position hold 0.6667. These same-set scores are explicitly labeled development/selection statistics, not held-out evidence; this vector was not applied retrospectively to the 7,500-run benchmark. The earlier 864-episode protocol remains distinct.
- The Stage-1 outcome table (4,320 rows), per-episode hash index (4,320 hashes), eight-artifact source hash register, manifests, and recovery audit are preserved in the supplement/package. A read-only comparison found all eight copied artifacts matched the original SHA-256 register and all three named sidecars matched; the two row tables each contain 4,320 records. The original episode MAT checkpoints remain unchanged in `results/v12a_pooled_weight_development_inclusive_dev48_20261012/episodes/` and are not duplicated in the ZIP.
- Stage-2 calibration and the planned seven-method held-out controller results are not reported in this release. All findings are simulation-only with prescribed obstacle tracks and point forecasts; no real-flight validation, uncertainty-calibration, global-optimality, or universal-superiority claim is made.

## Remaining limitations

The five-controller aggregate is historical and uses 1-s DWA screening, whereas Stage-1 uses a separate 3-s development configuration. The Stage-1 selected vector and development score cannot be treated as validation of the historical ranking. The local no-forecast comparator is an architecture-level reconstruction because the source article does not supply executable dynamic-obstacle prediction details. Results remain limited to simulation, prescribed obstacle motion and the tested controller implementations.

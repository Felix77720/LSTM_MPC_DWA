\begin{abstract}
First, UAVs must avoid moving obstacles in three dimensions even when turns, acceleration and pauses make their motion hard to predict. Second, we couple model predictive control (MPC) and the dynamic window approach (DWA) through one causal long short-term memory (LSTM) forecast: MPC uses it to plan a route, while DWA screens reachable actions and bounds MPC's first command. Then, in a frozen 500-scene comparison with a 1-s DWA screen, shared LSTM reached the goal without collision in 936/1,500 runs (62.4\%), versus 836/1,500 (55.7\%) when only DWA received LSTM. The paired difference was 6.7 percentage points (95\% scene-cluster confidence interval, 4.2--9.2). In a separate 100-scene evaluation with a 3-s screen, sharing reached 73.7\%; its contrast with DWA-only LSTM was inconclusive, while standalone MPC-CV had the highest rate, 92.0\%. Finally, the simulations identify a protocol-specific benefit of forecast sharing and a protocol-dependent controller ranking; flight validation remains future work.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
UAV, 3D path planning, dynamic obstacles, LSTM, model predictive control, dynamic window approach
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
Unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) must avoid obstacles that move during flight. Turns, acceleration and pauses make recent positions unreliable guides to what happens next. Model predictive control (MPC) optimizes a short future route, whereas the dynamic window approach (DWA) selects a feasible next action \cite{mayne2000,fox1997}. Predictive UAV planners already use obstacle-motion forecasts in route optimization \cite{olcay2024,xu2025}; here we test where one causal long short-term memory (LSTM) forecast should enter a coupled MPC--DWA loop. The main comparison tests whether also giving MPC the forecast already used by DWA changes outcomes; CV-only, MPC-only-LSTM, DWA-only-LSTM and standalone arms provide context.

\section{Problem Formulation}
The planning scene is a finite three-dimensional workspace with a start and a goal. The UAV and each moving obstacle are represented by spheres, and each obstacle follows a trajectory generated before the run, independently of the UAV. The planner observes the UAV's current state and the obstacles' current states and histories, but cannot access their future true positions. Figure~\ref{fig:scenario} defines this geometry; recorded planner paths are shown separately.

At each replanning step, the planner chooses a command containing desired speed, pitch rate and yaw rate. It predicts motion over a rolling horizon but applies only the next update's screened action before observing the scene again and replanning. The task is to reach the goal while remaining inside the flight corridor and avoiding contact with every obstacle along the trajectory. An episode ends at goal arrival, when the planner has no screened action to issue, or at the simulator's stopping limit.

\section{Planning Method}
At each update, the planner reads the UAV state and obstacle history, then produces a causal forecast from observations available so far. At startup, MPC first solves with this forecast to initialize its route and control sequence; thereafter, the previous MPC solution is shifted forward to provide the guide. The planner computes trust from that guide, then allocates DWA samples between MPC-guided and exploratory controls. It rolls out each candidate and applies clearance, corridor and braking gates before scoring the survivors. The highest-scoring safe action is selected; if optional lateral recentering is triggered, the adjusted action is rolled out and screened again, with failure reverting to the original screened action.

If the normal safe set is empty, the planner generates emergency candidates and screens them against the emergency clearance, corridor and braking conditions. It rechecks the selected emergency action before use; if none passes, the simulator stops without issuing an unchecked command. When the normal safe set is nonempty, the component-wise control extrema of its safe candidates form a box that constrains MPC's first command on re-solve. MPC's solution guides the next cycle, but the screened DWA or emergency action is the one executed for the next update. Figure~\ref{fig:algorithm} summarizes the loop.

\subsection{Core equations}
The equations below define the vehicle update, obstacle forecast and planner decisions. The vehicle state contains position, speed, pitch and yaw. The signed distance between its collision sphere and obstacle $j$ is
\begingroup\small
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf h_t&=[\cos\theta_t\cos\psi_t,\cos\theta_t\sin\psi_t,\sin\theta_t]^{\mathsf T},\\
\mathbf p_{t+1}&=\mathbf p_t+\Delta t\,v_t\mathbf h_t,\\
v_{t+1}&=v_t+\frac{\Delta t}{\tau_v}(v_{c,t}-v_t),\\
\theta_{t+1}&=\theta_t+\Delta t\,\omega_{\theta,t},\quad
\psi_{t+1}=\psi_t+\Delta t\,\omega_{\psi,t},\\
\hat d_{j,k}&=\|\mathbf p_{t+k|t}-\hat{\mathbf o}_{j,t+k|t}\|_2-r_u-r_j.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
\endgroup
Here $t$ is the current step, $k$ a future step, and $j=1,\ldots,M$ indexes obstacles. The unit direction $\mathbf h_t$ follows pitch $\theta_t$ and yaw $\psi_t$. The update period is $\Delta t=0.1$ s; $\tau_v$ is the speed-response time; and $v_c,\omega_\theta,\omega_\psi$ are commanded speed and angular rates. In the clearance term, $\mathbf p_{t+k|t}$ and $\hat{\mathbf o}_{j,t+k|t}$ are predicted positions. The radii are $r_u$ and $r_j$. Positive $\hat d_{j,k}$ means separation, zero contact and negative overlap.

At each update, the causal LSTM \cite{hochreiter1997} maps ten obstacle observations to thirty 3-D offsets from the latest observed centre:
\begingroup\small
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf q_{j,s}&=[\mathbf o_{j,s}^{\mathsf T},\mathbf v_{j,s}^{\mathsf T},\cos\chi_{j,s},\sin\chi_{j,s}]^{\mathsf T},\\
\hat{\mathbf o}^{\rm LSTM}_{j,t+k|t}&=\mathbf o^{\rm obs}_{j,t}+[f_{\Theta}(\mathbf q_{j,t-9:t})]_k.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:forecast}
\end{equation}
\endgroup
Here $s$ indexes history and $k=1,\ldots,30$ indexes forecast steps. The observed position $\mathbf o$ and velocity $\mathbf v$ are followed by sine and cosine of heading $\chi$, giving eight input features. The trained network $f_\Theta$ outputs one 3-D offset per future step, and $[\cdot]_k$ selects offset $k$. Ten observations span 0.9 s and the forecast spans 3 s. Each offset is anchored to the latest observed centre, not cumulatively summed. At startup, the earliest state fills missing history. Constant-velocity (CV) baselines extrapolate from the latest two positions, using zero initial velocity when only one is available; position hold repeats the latest centre. Training details are in Supplementary Methods.

MPC uses the forecast over its 30-step horizon to minimize goal error, control effort and changes, route deviation, and obstacle-clearance penalties:
\begingroup\small
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{\mathbf u_{1:30}}J_{\rm MPC}={}&
\sum_{k=0}^{29}w_p\|\mathbf p_{t+k|t}-\mathbf g\|_2^2\\
&+w_f\|\mathbf p_{t+30|t}-\mathbf g\|_2^2+w_rJ_r\\
&+\sum_{k=1}^{30}w_u\|\mathbf u_k\|_2^2
+w_{\Delta u}\sum_{k=2}^{30}\|\mathbf u_k-\mathbf u_{k-1}\|_2^2\\
&+\sum_{k=1}^{30}\sum_j
\left(w_o[-\hat d_{j,k}]_+^2+w_s[d_{\rm soft}-\hat d_{j,k}]_+^2\right),\\
\text{s.t. }&\mathbf x_{t+k|t}=f(\mathbf x_{t+k-1|t},\mathbf u_k),\quad
\mathbf u_1\in\mathcal U_t\ \text{if }\mathcal A_t\ne\varnothing.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:mpc}
\end{equation}
\endgroup
Here $\mathbf u_{1:30}$ is the planned command sequence, $\mathbf x$ is the position--pitch--yaw--speed state, $\mathbf g$ is the goal, and $f$ is the state transition. Each command is $\mathbf u_k=[v_{c,k},\omega_{\theta,k},\omega_{\psi,k}]^{\mathsf T}$, containing desired speed, pitch rate and yaw rate. The weights $w_p,w_f,w_r,w_u,w_{\Delta u},w_o,w_s$ penalize, respectively, stage goal error, terminal goal error, route deviation, command magnitude, command changes, predicted overlap and clearance below $d_{\rm soft}=0.35$ m. Here $J_r$ is the sum of squared cross-track deviations from the direct start-to-goal line. The positive-part operator is $[x]_+=\max(0,x)$. These are optimization costs; overlap and clearance are soft penalties. The vehicle dynamics, physical command limits and per-step command-change limits remain hard constraints throughout the horizon. When DWA has safe candidates, only MPC's first command is additionally constrained to their expanded control box. If the normal safe set is empty, MPC uses its ordinary physical bounds to update the next guide, while execution remains with the screened DWA or emergency action. Full definitions and limits are in Supplementary Methods S1.1.

Trust allocates DWA samples between guided and exploratory actions using safety, goal progress, agreement with the previous MPC guide, and control smoothness:
\begingroup\small
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\tau&=\sigma_s\tau_{\rm sa}+\sigma_g\tau_g+\sigma_t\tau_{\rm ref}+\sigma_m\tau_{\rm sm},\\
\tau_{\rm ref}&=\exp(-\alpha_T\Delta_p),\\
B&=\operatorname{round}[300(1+\alpha e^{-\beta\tau})],\\
B_g&=\operatorname{round}(B\tau),\quad B_e=B-B_g.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:trust}
\end{equation}
\endgroup
The four trust components summarize the MPC reference available before DWA sampling. Safety is its minimum predicted clearance; goal progress is the reduction in distance to the goal from the reference's first to last point; reference match decreases with the distance $\Delta_p$ from the current UAV position to the preceding MPC reference's first point; and smoothness reflects changes across the planned controls. Each component is normalized to $[0,1]$. The nonnegative coefficients $\sigma_s,\sigma_g,\sigma_t,\sigma_m$ weight safety, goal progress, reference match and smoothness, respectively, and sum to one. Thus $\tau$ is also in $[0,1]$ and is a normalized sampling heuristic. In $\tau_{\rm ref}=\exp(-\alpha_T\Delta_p)$, $\alpha_T$ sets the exponential decay of reference match with distance. In the budget equation, $\alpha$ is the sample-budget gain and $\beta$ controls budget sensitivity to trust; both are fixed implementation settings selected independently of Stage 2. Higher trust raises the guided fraction while lowering the total budget. The 4,320-episode development sweep selected $[0.15,0.15,0.15,0.55]$ by the equal-weight mean arrival rate across five integrated arms (0.8042 in that development set). The historical 1-s benchmark instead retained inherited weights $[0.35,0.30,0.15,0.20]$, untuned on its 500 scenes. Full score definitions are in Supplementary Methods S1.2.

DWA rejects unsafe rollouts, scores the survivors, and uses their control range to bound MPC's first input:
\begingroup\small
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal A_t&=\{b\in\mathcal B_t:d_{\min}^{(b)}>0.25~\mathrm m,\ \mathcal C_b=1,\\
&\hspace{1em}(v_{\rm end}^{(b)})^2\leq2a_{\max}d_{\min}^{(b)}\},\quad
b^*=\arg\max_{b\in\mathcal A_t}S_b,\\
u_{i,t}^{-}&=\max\!\left(u_i^{\min},\min_{b\in\mathcal A_t}u_i^{(b)}-\delta\right),\\
u_{i,t}^{+}&=\min\!\left(u_i^{\max},\max_{b\in\mathcal A_t}u_i^{(b)}+\delta\right),\\
\mathcal U_t&=\prod_{i=1}^{3}[u_{i,t}^{-},u_{i,t}^{+}],\quad \delta=0.1.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:dwa}
\end{equation}
\endgroup
Here $b$ indexes a candidate, $\mathcal B_t$ is the sampled set, and $\mathcal A_t$ contains candidates passing all three gates. The minimum surface clearance $d_{\min}^{(b)}$ is evaluated at discrete, time-aligned rollout states against forecast obstacle positions and must exceed $0.25$ m. The indicator $\mathcal C_b=1$ means the entire candidate rollout stays within the absolute altitude bounds and relative route-altitude corridor. Its terminal speed is $v_{\rm end}^{(b)}$; $a_{\max}=1.0$ m s$^{-2}$ is the available braking deceleration in the stopping-distance gate. The score ranks survivors by endpoint position, heading and speed agreement with the MPC reference, predicted obstacle clearance and vertical profile. For a nonempty safe set, index $i$ denotes desired speed, pitch rate or yaw rate; $u_i^{(b)}$ is candidate $b$'s command in that channel, bounded physically by $u_i^{\min}$ and $u_i^{\max}$. The shared numerical expansion $\delta=0.1$ is applied in each channel's native units, despite their different dimensions, then clipped to those physical limits. The resulting box constrains only MPC's first input; safety remains with the screened rollout that is executed. If optional lateral recentering fails its re-screen, the original screened action is retained. When no normal candidate passes, emergency actions are screened and rechecked; if none passes, the simulator stops. Reported collision-free arrivals are then classified with a continuous piecewise-linear swept-path audit, which checks between the discrete rollout states used by the online gates. Full definitions are in Supplementary Methods S1.2--S1.4.

\subsection{How the shared forecast is used}
Both planners receive the same causal LSTM forecast, but use it at different stages: MPC evaluates it across its route horizon, while DWA checks forecast positions along each candidate rollout. This design aligns the route guide and local action screen on the same predicted obstacle positions. Giving the forecast to MPC therefore changes the guide and can also change DWA's guided/exploratory sample mix through trust. The frozen paired comparison measures this closed-loop sharing effect relative to DWA-only LSTM under its 1-s screen. Component-level attribution would require additional ablations; in the separate 3-s evaluation, the corresponding contrast was inconclusive. Supplementary Methods S1.4 gives the executed cycle and fallback logic.

\section{Simulation and Results}
We report the frozen matched comparison first and the separate exploratory evaluation second; their protocols and scene banks are not pooled.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.86\columnwidth]{seven_method_outcomes_main.pdf}
\caption{Collision-free goal-arrival rates in two separate protocols. (a) Frozen five-controller benchmark: 500 scenes, three matched seeds, $n=1{,}500$ per method and a 1-s DWA screen. (b) Separate seven-method exploration: 100 scenes, three matched seeds, $n=300$ per method and a 3-s screen. Bars are point estimates; the paired shared-minus-DWA-only contrast is shown above each panel: +6.7 percentage points (95\% scene-cluster CI 4.2--9.2) in (a), and -1.0 points (simultaneous 95\% CI -4.8--2.8) in (b). The right column gives swept contacts for each method. The highest-rate method differs by protocol; panels are not pooled.}
\label{fig:rates}
\end{figure}

Under this frozen comparison, both arms gave DWA LSTM, but only the shared arm gave the forecast to MPC. Shared LSTM achieved 936/1,500 (62.4\%) safe arrivals, versus 836/1,500 (55.7\%) for DWA-only LSTM. The paired gain was 6.7 percentage points (95\% scene-cluster CI, 4.2--9.2). These 3,000 matched runs are included in the 7,500-run benchmark, not an additional sample.

The separate exploratory evaluation comprised 2,100 episodes across seven arms, 100 scenes and three matched seeds per scene. It used 0.1-s updates, 3-s MPC/DWA horizons and a 650-step limit. Arrival required goal tolerance without swept-path contact. Seeds were averaged within scene; 20,000 scene-cluster bootstrap resamples formed simultaneous max-|t| intervals for six contrasts. Shared LSTM arrived in 221/300 runs (73.7\%); standalone MPC-CV had the highest rate, 276/300 (92.0\%), with 15 swept contacts. The shared-versus-DWA-only contrast was inconclusive (-1.0 percentage point; simultaneous 95\% CI, -4.8 to 2.8). Sharing exceeded position hold by 16.3 points (95\% CI, 5.1--27.6) and was 18.3 points below MPC-CV (95\% CI, -29.6 to -7.0). Controller rankings therefore remained protocol-dependent (Fig.~\ref{fig:rates}b; Supplementary Table S12).

Within the frozen comparison, standalone DWA+LSTM and MPC+LSTM reached 82.4\% and 95.2\%, respectively, while position hold reached 2.2\% (Fig.~\ref{fig:rates}a; Supplementary Methods S4). Thus, the shared forecast improved on DWA-only input in this paired protocol, while the standalone baselines recorded higher aggregate rates.

\section{Discussion}
Forecast sharing raised safe arrival in the frozen 1-s comparison, whereas the 3-s exploration produced an inconclusive shared-versus-DWA-only contrast and ranked standalone MPC-CV highest. The protocols also differed in scene banks, trust weights and horizons, so their ranking change motivates a controlled sensitivity study rather than a pooled estimate. LSTM used simulator-recorded obstacle velocity and heading, whereas CV estimated velocity from positions; the comparisons therefore evaluate complete configured prediction pipelines. Prescribed obstacle tracks, point forecasts and simulation-only evaluation define the present scope, with uncertainty-aware prediction and flight tests as the next validation steps.
\section{Conclusion}
In simulation, shared LSTM increased collision-free arrival by 6.7 percentage points over DWA-only input in the frozen 500-scene, 1-s DWA comparison. In the separate 3-s evaluation, standalone MPC-CV had the highest rate, while the shared-versus-DWA-only contrast was inconclusive.
